MISSION PLANNER // SOUTH POLE

Compare lunar sunlight and potential Earth visibility using built-in JPL Horizons DE441 data for the demo sites, NASA LRO LOLA terrain horizons, and a labelled offline fallback for unmatched dates/sites.

Solar energy is a direct-beam estimate for the entered panel tilt and azimuth. It accounts for the terrain skyline, partial solar-disc visibility and the Sun's apparent angular diameter as a distance proxy. It excludes thermal effects, degradation, diffuse/reflected light, wiring and power-electronics losses, shadowing by lander hardware and detailed array-control behavior. Antenna elevation is a configurable geometric mask above the local terrain/geometric horizon; it does not prove an RF link.

Both demo sites are preloaded with built-in JPL Horizons Sun/Earth ephemerides and NASA LRO LOLA terrain-horizon profiles for 14 Nov 2026, 00:00–24:00 UTC. Matching site and start time are required for JPL data.

Scientific limitations & data provenance
The provenance panel above reports the source metadata declared by the imported terrain profile, including its dataset, resolution, site coordinates, horizon sampling and processing method. Terrain masking is applied only when a matching horizon profile is imported; otherwise the planner explicitly falls back to the geometric horizon. Matching built-in JPL Horizons (DE441) topocentric Sun/Earth ephemerides are used for the preloaded 14 Nov 2026 scenarios; other dates or coordinates fall back to the labelled approximate offline model unless matching data are imported. LOLA terrain horizons are DEM-derived estimates, not certified landing-safety assessments. Earth-disc visibility is geometric line-of-sight; antenna-masked windows apply a user-selected elevation mask but are not a confirmed RF link. Solar energy is a direct-beam estimate for the selected panel orientation and includes partial-disc terrain masking, but is not a detailed spacecraft power-system simulation.
